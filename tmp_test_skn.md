### Root Word Meaning
The root "سكن" (sakana) fundamentally means the stopping of movement or the settling of something after being in motion. This core idea branches into several related concepts: peaceful stillness, residing in a place, and finding comfort or tranquility. For example, when one "sakan fulān makān kadhā," it means they settled down and resided in that place. The root also implies quietness, as in "sakana al-rajul" (the man became silent) or when the wind or anger "sakana" (calmed down). Al-Raghib highlights the idea of stability after movement, which extends to the notion of dwelling or inhabiting a place, with "maskan" being the place of dwelling. The idea of something that brings stillness or rest, such as a "sakana" (a source of peace or comfort) or a "sakkān" (a rudder that stabilizes a ship), further illustrates this primary meaning.

### Quranic Usage
In the Quran, the root "سكن" is used to convey a sense of dwelling, peace, and cessation. For instance, Allah mentions "maskanah" (dwelling places) in ﴿لا يُرى إِلَّا مَساكِنُهُمْ﴾ [Al-Ahqaf : 25], referring to ruined homes. The concept of rest and residence is seen in ﴿وَلَهُ ما سَكَنَ فِي اللَّيْلِ وَالنَّهارِ﴾ [Al-An'am : 13], meaning all that rests or exists in the night and day, and in ﴿لِتَسْكُنُوا فِيهِ﴾ [Yunus : 67], indicating the purpose of night for human rest. The word "sakanan" (a source of tranquility) is used when Allah describes homes as "sakanan" for people in ﴿وَاللَّهُ جَعَلَ لَكُمْ مِنْ بُيُوتِكُمْ سَكَناً﴾ [An-Nahl : 80] and the night as "sakanan" in ﴿وَجَعَلَ اللَّيْلَ سَكَناً﴾ [Al-An'am : 96]. "Sakīnah" (tranquility or peace of mind) appears in verses like ﴿أَنْزَلَ السَّكِينَةَ فِي قُلُوبِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ﴾ [Al-Fath : 4], signifying a divinely bestowed calm. Even the word "miskin" (poor person) is linked to this root, implying someone who lacks the means to settle or be stable.
