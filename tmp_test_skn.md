### Root Word Meaning

The root "ودد" fundamentally conveys the concept of profound affection, often intertwined with a fervent desire or yearning. As Al-Raghib highlights, it encompasses both "محبّة الشيء" (love for something) and "تمنّي كونه" (the wish for its existence or occurrence). This means that to "ودد" something is to both love it and to wish deeply for it, suggesting a close relationship between heartfelt affection and a longing for its presence or fulfillment. The *Lisan al-Arab* reinforces this, noting "الودُّ الحُبُّ" (al-wudd is love) and that the verb "وددتُ" often signifies "تمنّيتُ" (I wished or longed for), indicating a powerful emotional attachment that expresses itself in active desire.

### Quranic Usage

From this semantic core, the Quranic lexicon richly develops, manifesting the root "ودد" through various derivatives that signify profound emotional and interpersonal connections, or intense desires. Terms like "مَوَدَّة" (mawaddah), meaning love or affection, are frequently used to describe divinely instituted bonds, as seen in Surah Ar-Rum 30:21: "وَجَعَلَ بَيْنَكُمْ مَوَدَّةً وَرَحْمَةً" (He placed between you affection and mercy). Furthermore, the divine attribute "الودود" (al-Wadūd), meaning "the All-Loving," such as in Surah Al-Buruj 85:14 ("وَهُوَ الْغَفُورُ الْوَدُودُ"), encapsulates God's immense love and kindness towards His creation. The root also captures human longing or wishing, often in contexts of desire for something to happen or for a specific state, like in Surah Al-Baqarah 2:109 where it describes the wish of some People of the Book: "وَدَّ كَثِيرٌ مِنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ" (Many of the People of the Book wish...).
